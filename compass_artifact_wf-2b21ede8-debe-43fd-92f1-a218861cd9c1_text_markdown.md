# Singapore Boutique Compliance Consulting Firms — Sales Prospect List for ComplianceAssist

## TL;DR
- We identified **24 named Singapore compliance consulting firms** that serve wealth managers, asset managers, fund managers and other MAS-regulated financial institutions — of which roughly **12–14 are true small boutiques (under ~30 staff)** that fit the ComplianceAssist ICP almost perfectly. The strongest near-term targets are **Integrity Consulting, Ingenia Consultants, Ethikom Consultancy, HM (Holland & Marie), Integrium, RT Compliance, Alder, Singapore Compliance (compliance.com.sg), AML Singapore, and Alba Compliance**.
- Every firm on the list is reachable: each has a website plus at least one of email, WhatsApp, phone or LinkedIn. Of the 24, **18 publish a direct email**, **20 publish a phone or WhatsApp number**, and **22 have a LinkedIn company page**.
- The Singapore market is structurally well-suited to ComplianceAssist: MAS explicitly permits compliance outsourcing for fund management companies with AUM under S$1 billion, and these boutiques typically serve 20–80 LFMC / VCFM / former-RFMC clients each on thin teams — exactly the use case where automated circular ingestion, a multi-regulator compliance calendar with escalation, and clause-level obligations mapping create the largest ROI.

## Key Findings
1. **The market has just consolidated, and the boutique layer is the resulting opportunity.** IQ-EQ acquired both ComplianceAsia and Lymon Pte Ltd on 27 June 2023, creating "a newly combined team of 101 FTE" across APAC and making IQ-EQ "the market leading independent Regulatory Compliance business globally" (per the IQ-EQ/Astorg press release of that date). Earlier, on 16 June 2021, Waystone announced "the creation of a new global compliance offering via the merging of four compliance service providers," one of which was Singapore's Argus Global. The net effect: small Singapore fund managers who used to default to ComplianceAsia or Argus are now actively shopping the surviving partner-led specialists (Integrity Consulting, Ingenia, Ethikom, HM, Integrium, RT Compliance). These post-consolidation boutiques cannot afford to build their own RegTech and are losing scale advantages to the consolidators — they are the highest-conviction prospects for ComplianceAssist.
2. **MAS regulatory cadence is intensifying.** Four near-simultaneous changes are forcing every boutique on this list to absorb and operationalise MAS material at high volume — the exact pain point ComplianceAssist's circular-summarisation and clause-level repository modules solve:
   - The **RFMC regime was formally repealed on 1 August 2024**, with existing RFMCs required to apply for A/I LFMC status by 30 June 2024 (per MAS's March 2024 Response to Consultation, confirmed by IQ-EQ and Waystone client alerts).
   - The **July 2025 MAS AML/CFT notice revisions** introduced three headline changes (per Allen & Gledhill's 30 June 2025 client alert): money laundering was explicitly redefined to include proliferation financing, making PF risk assessments mandatory; STR filing timelines were codified at 5 business days (1 business day for sanctions/PF cases); and CDD was expanded for trust relevant parties including protectors, classes of beneficiaries and objects of a power.
   - In **July 2025, MAS Deputy Chairman Chee Hong Tat publicly committed to cutting 13O/13U family-office tax-incentive approvals "to within three months"** (per Hubbis, 21 August 2025), alongside a plan to shorten ultra-HNW account opening at private banks — putting massive throughput pressure on family-office compliance advisors.
   - January 2026 enhanced CDD rules for Southeast-Asian-exposed PE managers add re-verification requirements at each new investment (per the Karman compliance guide).
3. **MAS Notice SFA04-N02, the Compliance Toolkit, and the multi-regulator overlay (MAS + IRAS + ACRA + CAD/STRO) create a natural fit for ComplianceAssist's Compliance Calendar + CTR engine.** Boutiques are currently running these in spreadsheets or generic project tools — Cambridge Advisers' and Ingenia's own marketing copy explicitly describes building "compliance calendars" and "compliance monitoring programmes" for every new client.
4. **There is a clear "AUM under S$1 bn" outsourcing window** built directly into MAS rules — these firms' core clients are the LFMC A/I, VCFM and ex-RFMC managers who are *required* to demonstrate adequate compliance arrangements but are too small to staff a full in-house team. The boutiques sit in that gap and would be ComplianceAssist's natural distribution channel.
5. **A second tier of mid-sized multi-disciplinary firms (Apex, Hawksford, Tricor Axcelasia, Acclime, Bestar, Forvis Mazars, IQ-EQ)** has Singapore compliance practices that exceed 30 staff and therefore fall outside the strict "small" criterion — but their *Singapore FMC compliance sub-teams* are typically under 20 people and would still be qualified partner-channel prospects.

## Recommendations

**Stage 1 — Outbound now (next 2 weeks):** Send personalised, founder-to-founder emails from a ChiStats principal to the 10 Tier A firms. Open with a one-paragraph reference to a recent MAS development — the July 2025 AML/CFT notice changes (proliferation-financing inclusion, 5-day STR codification, expanded trust-relevant-party CDD per Allen & Gledhill's 30 June 2025 alert), or the 1 August 2024 RFMC regime repeal. Offer a 30-minute walkthrough of (a) automated circular ingestion, (b) the multi-regulator compliance calendar, and (c) the clause-level repository — the three modules that map directly to what Ingenia, Cambridge Advisers and Bestar already describe on their own websites. Lead with the "20+ hours/month per compliance officer" metric and 100% obligation coverage. *Benchmark to advance:* if ≥3 of the 10 take a demo, move to Stage 2; if <3 respond, narrow messaging to a single use case (AML/CFT obligation mapping) and re-run.

**Stage 2 — Pilot offer (weeks 3–6):** Convert the most engaged Tier A firm (likely Ingenia, Cambridge Advisers, or Bestar based on their public RegTech-friendly stance) into a paid pilot covering 5–10 of their FMC clients, priced on a per-client SaaS basis. The pilot's success metric should be the same metric they already advertise to their clients — hours saved on ongoing monitoring and demonstrable evidence quality at audit. *Benchmark to advance:* one Tier A reference customer with a quotable testimonial = trigger Stage 3.

**Stage 3 — Channel partnerships (weeks 6–12):** Approach Tier B and Tier C firms (Cambridge Advisers, Tricor Axcelasia, Apex, RegPac, IQ-EQ via Philippa Allen) with a white-label or revenue-share partnership. These firms have 50–500+ FMC clients each; a single channel deal can deliver more seats than the entire Tier A direct effort. Lead with the reference customer from Stage 2.

**Always:** Verify the live status of every firm on the MAS Financial Institutions Directory and ACRA before sending high-stakes outreach. Two firms in this list have data-quality flags (Alba Compliance and Laven Partners SG) and should not be approached without a fresh check.

## Caveats
- **Team-size signals are approximate.** Most boutiques do not publish headcount; figures above are inferred from LinkedIn employee counts, "team" pages, and ACRA filings, not audited.
- **The "small" criterion is genuinely fluid.** Cambridge Advisers, Bestar and Tricor Axcelasia SG sit on the boundary of ~30 staff and may exceed it depending on how their parent groups are counted; included as Tier B because their *Singapore FMC compliance practice* is small.
- **Two firms have status flags requiring verification:** Alba Compliance's Singapore Pte Ltd shows "Struck Off" on ACRA, and Laven Partners' SG office may have been substantially scaled down following the group's pivot to UK ManCo/AR hosting. Verify before outreach.
- **The query asked for compliance consultancies, not in-house compliance teams.** All firms listed are external consultancies / advisors, not asset managers themselves. (Privium Fund Management appeared in search results but is itself a licensed LFMC and was excluded.)
- **A handful of well-known names did not surface in this round** because they market under personal brands, sit within larger law firms, or operate exclusively via word-of-mouth (e.g., ex-Big-4 partners now running sole-practitioner advisories). A second pass through the ICA Singapore and IBF-accredited trainer member lists would likely add another 5–10 names.
- **The MAS Financial Institutions Directory is the ground truth** for any firm's regulatory status. ComplianceAssist's pitch will be more credible if each prospect is re-verified against the FID register immediately before outreach.
